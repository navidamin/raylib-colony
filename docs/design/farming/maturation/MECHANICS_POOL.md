# Mechanics Forge — the candidate pool

**Status:** RAW POOL (generated 2026-09-05) · **Branch:** `claude/farming-unit-design-4rz536`
**Interactive:** [`mechanics_forge.html`](mechanics_forge.html) — deck / list / framings / research

154 candidate mechanics for the farming unit, produced by a multi-agent run: 14 design
lenses ideating and self-sharpening, grounded in 115 findings swept from real lunar and
Martian agriculture research, then put through three adversarial critics (fun, science,
buildability). 79 carry critic scores; the rest are unvetted. 14 were cut outright.

This file is the durable record. The HTML is how you browse it.

> Fun scores are the mean of three harsh critics where 3 means *merely acceptable*.
> `revise` means at least one critic wants a change; the objection and the smallest fix
> are recorded in the interactive page.

## Synoptic framings

Single pictures that hold several branches at once — the same job the buffer↔speed frontier does for media.

- **THE TWO-FAILURE PLANE (the L)** — The frontier the project already found is only the bottom edge of a plane, because the roster is ordered by two different failures and the orderings are not the same. Blackout and night reorder the same media: SOIL survives a month of blackout and yields nothing through a night; VAT sails through the night on reactor watts and dies in hours if those watts stop. So a farm hedged against one is not hedged against the other — B5's "never put the whole farm in one medium" sharpens into "you need points on both strokes of the L, and the top shelf has its own x-spread, so lightless is not a synonym for safe." The bubble sizes add the second reading: every empty or fragile region of this plane is where the small bubbles are. Both failures take the salad, the vitamins and the mood long before they take the calories, because H9 anticorrelates calories with speed. A blackout is a diet-quality and morale emergency first and a famine last — which is backwards from the intuition the player arrives with, and it makes the correct emergency action (spend stored power on the greens, not the potatoes) genuinely non-obvious.  
  _Unifies:_ B (all 13 media, B4 trade-off table, B5 portfolio rule, B6 microbial ceiling as a cap on VAT's bubble), H9 and H4 via bubble size and the morale/micronutrient cluster, C2/C4 (the six programs read as postures in this plane; FORTNIGHT is the y-axis made mandatory), G1 and G3 (both threats are power politics).
- **THE LOOM** — Rotation quality is a texture, not a table — which is exactly what C3's implicitness principle needs and has been missing an object for. A staggered, well-formed register weaves a diagonal: harvest ticks march steadily down-right and the kcal strip is flat. A badly formed one bands vertically: everything ripens at once, the kcal strip becomes glut-then-famine, and — the part you cannot see any other way — every plot is simultaneously at the same phase, so a single pathogen or outage takes the whole cohort. Vertical banding is a risk pattern and a supply pattern at the same time, and the picture says so without a single combinatorial statement. Two more things fall out for free. A cut-and-come-again crop draws as one solid rail across the entire width, which explains "never frees its plot" better than any tooltip. And the dark block's bars always sit offset to the right of the harvests that fed them, making H8's two-registers-sharing-a-clock a visible phase lag — the number nobody has yet named is how many days after a wheat harvest the MYCO output actually arrives, and the loom is where you read it. During the night slab the light rows go flat while the dark rows keep running: FORTNIGHT is literally legible as a fabric with two thread colours.  
  _Unifies:_ C entire (C1's rotation invariant, C2's six programs, C3's implicitness, C4's time binding, C5's recognition moment) held together with D (D1 plots, D3 time-made-spatial, D5 stakes), plus A1.c's cycle phases, H1.d harvest modes, H8's alphabet handoff, G4's food ledger and spoilage, E3's cycle lens.
- **THE HABITABILITY GAP** — The spectrum tax, the CO₂ tax and the humidity tax are not three flavour details; they are one law seen three times. Plant optimum lies outside the human envelope on every axis at once, so yield and habitability are opposed by construction rather than by balance choice. The consequence is that a farm cannot be one room: it necessarily partitions into rooms the crew may stand in and rooms they may not, and that partition is spatial, costs airlock mass and shift scheduling, and puts the farm's two products in direct conflict — the calories come from the volume nobody can enter, the morale comes from the volume they can. PILGRIM and DEEP REGISTER are not flavour texts, they are two ends of one axis. And the sharpest reading is what is absent: nothing on the chart moves up and to the right. No crop choice, no medium, no chemistry does. The only thing that does is substituting machines for crew-hours, which reframes automation from a convenience tier into the mechanism that unlocks the productive half of the space — and gives E4's tier arc and the AI hook a reason to exist beyond saving clicks.  
  _Unifies:_ B2.d's spectrum tax and B2.e's CO₂ and ethylene material, F4's atmosphere and gas loops, G2 (crew morale, crew labour), G3 (watts), D2/D4 (zoning and adjacency as the spatial consequence), E3/E4 (which lens and which tier), and A1.a's gates — every point on this chart is a gate, never a draw.
- **THE GROWTH WEDGE** — Food is a floor and nitrogen is a ceiling, and both are proportional to crew — so they are two rays from the same origin, and the colony must live in the angle between them. That single geometric fact carries the design's hardest claim: if the nitrogen ray's slope is shallower than the calorie ray's, the wedge is empty at every scale, no colony size works at all, and only the parallel offset of imported, finite, leaking nitrogen makes existence possible. Early game is therefore not filling a food bar, it is widening a wedge. The second reading is that the binding constraint is almost never food after the first hour — the ceiling is nitrogen, or watts, or labour — so "we are hungry, build another farm" is usually the wrong move, and the wedge names the right one. The third is that the whole design becomes one question per choice: which ray does this rotate? Legumes and the crew loop rotate nitrogen up. MYCO and INSECT rotate the calorie floor down by eating the inedible half without touching light. VAT converts watts to calories with no photosynthesis in the path, easing the power lid — and B6's 20–25 g/day nucleic-acid ceiling appears here as a hard dashed cap on how far that rotation may go, which is the most satisfying possible home for that fact. Automation rotates the labour ceiling, which is the same lever the Habitability Gap identified, arriving from a different direction.  
  _Unifies:_ G entire (G1 life-support ledger, G2 crew as input, G3 power politics, G4 food ledger), A1.e couplings and A1.a's draws-versus-gates, F3/F4's nutrient and atmosphere loops, B2.b's nitrogen sourcing, B6's microbial ceiling, H3's service crops and H4's diet ledger, and B's media, each of which rotates a specific ray.
- **THE ATOM LEDGER — one bar that refuses to grow** — Standing biomass is nitrogen you cannot use. A lush, full farm is a farm that has immobilised its entire nutrient stock in leaves, and the colony cannot plant one more plot until the previous cohort is harvested, eaten and mineralised. Farm size therefore expands in discrete steps paced by the return leg of the loop, not continuously by area, light or power — and the natural growth curve is a staircase with a lag, which is a much more interesting economy than a throughput slider. It also gives the honest answer to 'why does my closed farm still need imports': because the tail exists. Two side payoffs: the bar teaches conservation with no tutorial (players learn it by watching something refuse to grow), and it is an executable invariant — if the total drifts without an import or a vent event, that is a bug, which matters in a codebase whose most expensive bug class is quantity-vs-fraction confusion.  
  _Unifies:_ B2.b (N from the crew loop, K+P from KREEP), F3 nutrient chemistry, F4 waste loops, G1 life-support ledger and G2 crew-as-input, H1.c residueMass and edibleFraction, A1.e couplings, and C — because rotation is literally the schedule by which N moves between segments.
- **SIX BROKEN RINGS — the price of the last percent** — You never close a loop; you choose how expensive its gap is, and the optimum closure is strictly less than 1 — it depends on mission horizon, not on ambition. Closing the last tenth of a loop costs more delivered mass than importing the leak for twenty years, so 'close everything' is a losing play and the game can say so with numbers. The deeper reframe: media stop being producers and become gap-closers. You do not build AQUA for fish, you build it because it is the only thing that narrows the nitrogen gap; you build HALO because the brine gap is the one nothing else touches. That converts B3's thirteen-item list into a build order derived from your own leak profile rather than from a tech tree. The code already contains the scalar version of this idea and does not know it: the Core unit's Life Support module carries a single `LoopClosure` parameter that scales its O2 and WATER draw by (1 - closure). This framing is simply: un-scalarise LoopClosure into six.  
  _Unifies:_ B3's second axis made literal (the roster is already sorted by 'which loop it closes' — this is that axis drawn), F4 atmosphere and waste loops, G1, G3 power politics (closure is paid in watts and mass), A1.e, and the whole C2 program map, since each of the six programs is a different choice about which gaps to tolerate.
- **THE CASCADE — residue is the main product** — By mass, the farm's largest product is not food — it is residue, and for most of the catalog the residue arrow is thicker than the food arrow. So decomposers are not a flavour branch: without them roughly half to four-fifths of every harvest becomes a disposal problem and the nitrogen locked inside it never returns to the Atom Ledger's left-hand segments. The mechanical consequence is one I have not seen stated anywhere in the existing docs and it is a genuinely non-obvious coupling: choosing all high-harvest-index crops starves the second half of your farm. An all-lettuce hydroponic operation is maximally efficient at converting light to salad and produces almost no feedstock, so its MYCO bay idles and its INSECT bin — the only realistic source of fat and B12 — goes hungry. Wheat is worth planting partly because 55% of it is straw. That makes crop selection a decomposer-feedstock decision as well as a calorie decision, and it turns B3's portfolio (things in parallel) into a cascade (things in series), which is a different and better object. It also fixes VAT's position honestly: VAT is the only step that can inject mass into the loop from outside biology (watts plus mined carbon and hydrogen), so it is the cascade's make-up valve, not a competitor to the greenhouse.  
  _Unifies:_ B3's three trophic families in one object, B6's ceiling, F1/F2/F3 biology, H1.c residueMass, H3's service crops, G4's food ledger. It is also the reason B3.c exists at all, drawn rather than argued.
- **THE RESIDENCE-TIME LADDER — buffers against events on one log axis** — The buffer↔speed frontier is one row of a larger table: a colony survives exactly the events that are shorter than its buffers, and failure latency is just τ under a specific outage. Drawing all stocks and all events at once produces the finding the farm branch needs most — the farm's own production latency, 60 to 150 days per crop cycle, sits to the right of nearly every buffer it feeds. The farm therefore cannot respond to a crisis; it can only have been sized for one three months earlier. That is an argument about what farming controls should even be: 'produce more, now' is a lie the interface should not offer, and the real levers are hold more (buffer, which is mass) or plant differently in advance (the register). It is the strongest justification C has for being the actual gameplay rather than a throughput dashboard. It also systematises guide §8: for every stock the release valve is one of exactly two moves — push τ right by adding buffer mass, or pull the event line left by shortening the outage — and the 14-day night is the one line no amount of buffer beats, which is precisely why FORTNIGHT's dark alphabet has to be organisms rather than plants.  
  _Unifies:_ B2.f failure latency, B4's trade-off table and B5's portfolio rule (all three are this picture restricted to one stock and one event), C4 day/night, G1, G3, H1.d cycle length, H6 seed viability, H9. It is the general law of which those are the special cases.
- **THE DURATION AXIS — everything in this farm is a clock** — Three, all geometric rather than argued. (1) B5's portfolio rule is DERIVED, not authored: only SOIL's failure-latency end reaches past the 14.8-day night line, so the night is not a lighting event, it is a latency event — and the lightless bars (MYCO, VAT) are the only ones whose left end is irrelevant because their input was never photons. (2) Each medium is an INTERVAL, not a point: failure latency and response latency are the same axis read from opposite ends, so H9's calories↔responsiveness becomes 'the bars march rightward together,' and a bar's width is how much of the farm's tempo that medium can cover alone — nothing covers it alone. (3) Two collisions with the actual engine, both actionable: AERO's 15–60 min failure latency is 0.2–0.8 TICKS, below the simulation's quantum, so fast-medium death cannot be a countdown and must be a per-tick hazard probability (or the tick must subdivide); and the shipped fertility decay (unit.cpp:339, 0.01 per deltaTime to a 0.2 floor) burns its entire range in 80 s = 4 GAME DAYS, five times faster than the fastest crop in the catalog — meaning no crop could ever complete above the floor. A table hides both; the axis shows them at a glance. Bonus: the attention stripe is a design ruler — anything whose period falls left of it must be automatable, anything right of a session must be delegable or it never resolves on screen, and E4's automation unlocks belong exactly where a mechanic's period crosses the stripe rather than at round tier numbers.  
  _Unifies:_ B (failure latency, the portfolio rule), H (crop cycles), C4 (the month/night), A1.c (cycle as a charter field), E4 and guide §11/§13 (tier arcs, ×20-sect scale, the AI hook), G1 (life support reserves as hours, not litres).
- **THE PHASE WHEEL — commensurability with the month** — Commensurability — and it splits the crop catalog on an axis nobody has drawn yet. A crop whose cycle is under half a month (<14.8 d) can live entirely inside the lunar day: free photons, zero lighting watts. NOTHING in the 17-crop catalog qualifies — mizuna at 21 d is the fastest. But SPROUT is 7 days, duckweed doubles in days, a MYCO flush is ~10–14: the 'emergency' media are precisely the ones whose period fits inside a lunar half-month. Their emergency role is a consequence of their clock, not a label pinned on them. Next: crops of 21–35 d — mizuna, radish, lettuce, basil, i.e. all the fast morale-heavy ones — are MONTH-LOCKED, because 30 d against 29.53 d drifts under half a day per cycle, so a plot planted at lunar sunrise harvests at lunar sunrise for a hundred cycles. The planting MOMENT is therefore a permanent decision, and it is invisible unless you draw it. Crops over ~35 d always straddle both halves: month-averaged, no scheduling escape from lighting watts, ever. So the fast/slow spine gains a third reading beside buffer↔speed and calories↔responsiveness: PHASE-CHOICE ↔ PHASE-AVERAGED. Fast crops hand you a lever; slow crops take it away. And a bare arc on the dial is a week with no harvest — the food-ledger dip appearing as a hole in a picture instead of a number falling later, which is exactly the surface C3's implicitness principle has been missing. Finally the unrolled strip buys a different currency with the same decision: spreading phases WITHIN the day flattens peak and cuts installed generator capacity (real load-diversity factor), while spreading ACROSS the month buys nothing, because the night is common-mode forcing that lifts every phototrophic plot at once. Fine staggering buys generator capacity; only storage mass or a lightless medium buys you out of the night.  
  _Unifies:_ C (the register), D3 (staggered planting as time-made-spatial), H1.d (cycle length and harvest mode), B2.d (DLI = PPFD × photoperiod), C4 (day/night model), G3 (power), G4 (food ledger), B5 (portfolio).
- **THE CREW WINDOW — two clocks, one room** — The binding constraint on a lunar farm is not water and not even power — it is SAFE CREW-HOURS inside a CO₂-enriched, red-blue-lit room, and the register schedules that resource as hard as it schedules plots. Three teeth. First, the ribbon's WIDTH IS A POWER COST: white light for inspection is watts spent on morale (the spectrum tax), and opening a window means drawing down and refilling CO₂. Widening the window to relieve congestion is a bill; narrowing it to save watts congests the tasks — a dial that trades in both directions. Second, the morale crops are the crew-hungry crops: tomato, pepper, strawberry — everything with high morale-per-gram and near-zero calories — needs HAND POLLINATION because there are no insects, so morale food costs crew-time and crew-time is part of what morale buys back. A loop with a sign, not a stat. Third and sharpest: a light in the plants' dark period is not neutral, it is a CONTROL INPUT. A night interruption suppresses flowering in short-day crops — this is literally how commercial growers hold chrysanthemums back — so 'turn the lights on so the crew can go in' can silently cost a flowering crop. That is the strongest possible version of A1.a's GATE: a requirement that consumes nothing and still bites. And it scales into a legible failure: a monoculture flowers together, so a wide planting of one fruiting crop dumps its whole pollination demand into one week and blows the window — a yield failure with no resource shortage behind it, whose fix (stagger, diversify families) is the same fix as everything else in this design.  
  _Unifies:_ G2 (crew, morale), B2.e (zoned atmospheres, time-boxed access), B2.d (the spectrum tax), F4 (gas loops), H1.b (crop gates) and the catalog's hand-pollination note, E2 (Fluids & Atmosphere station), C (the register scheduling people, not just plots), guide §10 and §11.
- **THE CLOSURE SPIRAL — does the plot come back?** — Sustainability, Eulerian closure, and 'does rotation order actually matter' turn out to be ONE question with an arithmetic answer: net drift around the circuit. Write the pair effect f(a→b) as the change in plot state when crop b follows crop a. If f is a potential difference — f(a→b) = g(b) − g(a) — then every closed circuit returns exactly to where it started and rotation ORDER is irrelevant to the outcome: the register is decoration, a button and not a mechanic. Order matters exactly to the extent the pair matrix is NON-CONSERVATIVE (has curl), or to the extent a floor or ceiling clips the path so intermediate minima bite. That hands the design a checkable test it does not currently have: sum the effects around a few small circuits in ASHFIELD's H×H conditioning matrix, and if they are all zero and nothing clips, the register has not yet become a mechanic. Second finding: the grand cycle has a computable length and it EXPLODES. A pair-register over n crops is a de Bruijn object of ~n² edges, so at n=4 with 30-day cycles the circuit closes in 480 game days; at n=16 it takes 7680 — twenty-six game-years, longer than the colony. Register width is therefore not free: n² × cycle is how long before a player ever sees closure, which argues for narrow per-plot lineages (n≈4–6) with diversity coming from DIFFERENT PLOTS ON DIFFERENT CIRCUITS rather than one wide circuit. Third, it renders the live bug: today's model is f = −0.01/s with a hard 0.2 floor and no positive term — a pure inward spiral that hits the wall in four game days and stops. The picture of the shipped game is a dot resting on the floor, which is the §8 violation the tree already lists, drawn.  
  _Unifies:_ B1.b (fertility as a real degrade/restore pair), C1 (the Eulerian / de Bruijn rotation), H8 (crop pairs as the register's alphabet), H3's service crops, F1–F3 (inoculants, nutrient chemistry, compost), H6/H7 (seed viability and pathogen load as other radii on the same picture), guide §8 (every filling or degrading thing needs a release valve).
- **THE SECTION — the farm cut open** — One vertical axis carries the entire farm, because a farm is a negotiation between a photon gradient descending and a water gradient ascending, with the crop living in the overlap. Drawn to scale, buffer stops being a stat and becomes AREA OF BLUE: you see at a glance which half of your farm is running on air, which is the buffer↔speed frontier rendered as inventory rather than asserted as a table. And every H2 verdict becomes geometry carrying its own reason — nothing needs to print INCOMPATIBLE, because the taproot visibly bottoms out and forks, and the peg visibly has nothing to push into. The 1/g capillary fringe drawn six times too tall makes the waterlogging problem self-evident and points at the substrate mill without a tutorial line.  
  _Unifies:_ B2.a/B2.c/B2.f and B3 (media, water inventory, failure latency) · H1.b/H2 (rootDepth, rootVolume, supportNeed, organFormation, the whole fit rule) · B2.d (light regime and spectrum) · D1/D3 (footprints, stagger) · B1.b and beneficiation's SIZE_SORT via the capillary fringe.
- **THE LADDER — two clocks on one axis** — Every plot is a long bet on a short fuse, and putting both clocks on one axis makes the ratio unmissable. Two things fall out that the tree currently keeps apart. First: the farm's real metric is the MINIMUM OF ITS DELIVERY SCHEDULE, not its total — a farm over-provisioned in aggregate with all its bar-ends clustered still starves the crew in week three, and that famine window is visible as a gap between bar-ends rather than a number nobody computes. Second, and sharper: spreading fuse lengths (B5's portfolio across media) and spreading harvest dates (D3's stagger across time) are THE SAME RULE ON TWO AXES OF ONE PICTURE, and the value-at-risk ramps show why — synchronised cohorts don't merely deliver in lumps, they concentrate everything you could lose into a single moment. Staggering is usually justified by continuous supply; its second justification is risk, and it is the same arithmetic as diversification. FORTNIGHT also stops being a described program and becomes a visible fact: only the lightless bars cross the grey band.  
  _Unifies:_ C1/C2/C3 (rhythm without combinatorics) · H1.d/H9 (cycle length, calories) · B2.f/B5 (latency, portfolio) · D3 (stagger) · G4 (food ledger, spoilage) · C4 (the night model, the moment it exists).
- **THE EXPOSURE PLANE — how many times you must not fail** — cycle ÷ latency is one derived number meaning HOW MANY CONSECUTIVE INTERVALS YOU MUST NOT FAIL IN ORDER TO BE PAID ONCE, and it converts two of the design's assertions into arithmetic. Soil potato asks you to survive about 5 hazard windows; aeroponic potato asks for about 5,000 — a factor of a thousand, which is why 'aeroponic potato is real but not how anyone feeds a colony' is true, and why the one real exception (seed tubers) is a deliberately short, non-calorie payout. Because marker size rises with y, exposure and stake grow together: the top-left corner holds the biggest bets on the shortest fuses, so you can gamble with leaves and cannot gamble with grain. That re-derives H9 instead of restating it, and it shows the existing triangle to be this plane's shadow — root geometry forbids some cells, but arithmetic quietly forbids many more that H2 calls WORKABLE.  
  _Unifies:_ B4/B5/B2.f × H1.d/H2/H9 — the buffer↔speed frontier and the crop–medium triangle plotted in a single coordinate system, with H1.b's DLI carried as the third channel.
- **THE HABITABILITY FRONTIER — the farm you cannot stand in** — Farm efficiency is denominated in human absence, and the frontier terminates at a tank — the most efficient possible farm has no room in it. That single reframing turns the six programs from a menu into positions on a curve, and it exposes a coupling stated nowhere in the tree: human presence is not only morale, it is the DISEASE SENSOR. H7's pathogens are caught by eyes on plants, and narrow-band light plus a twenty-minute access window is a farm that is efficient and blind at the same time. So every step rightward buys grams and buys latent, undetected risk — the same optimism as framing 2's fuses, paid in a different currency. And B6 is why the curve has a hard stop instead of an asymptote: the reason you cannot ride it to the end is uric acid, not a balance patch.  
  _Unifies:_ B2.d (the spectrum tax) · B2.e (CO₂ zoning, time-boxed access, transpiration humidity) · B3.b + B6 (chemotrophs and the ceiling) · G2 (morale, crew as an input not just a customer) · G3 (power politics) · C2 (PILGRIM and VAULT as the two ends of one curve rather than two flavours) · H7 (detection).
