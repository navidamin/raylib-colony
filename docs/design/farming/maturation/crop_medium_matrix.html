Crop × Medium Fit

Which crops each cultivation medium can actually grow — and why. The cells are not authored one by one; they are produced by a rule from crop traits and medium affordances, so every refusal has a reason a player can learn.

Media are sorted slow → fast, crops by root demand — and the filled cells collapse into a triangle. Soil grows everything; its cost is latency, mass and time, never capability. The fast media are specialists, and what they specialise in is crops that store nothing.

The fit matrix

Columns ordered by failure latency, slowest first. Rows ordered by how much root volume, depth and organ-formation space the crop demands. Every cell below is generated by the rule in the next panel — none is hand-placed.

The rule that generates it (H2)

A hand-written matrix is 136 numbers nobody can keep true. Traits in, compatibility out — so a new crop costs one row, not thirteen judgement calls.

Medium offers  depth · volume · anchoring
               allowsSwelling · allowsPegging · flooded

Crop needs     rootDepth · rootVolume · supportNeed
               organFormation ∈ { NONE, SWELLING, TUBER,
                                  GEOCARPIC, FLOODED }

fit(crop, medium)
  depth  = medium.depth     >= crop.rootDepth
  volume = medium.volume    >= crop.rootVolume
  anchor = medium.anchoring >= crop.supportNeed
  organ  = NONE
         | (SWELLING|TUBER && medium.allowsSwelling)
         | (GEOCARPIC      && medium.allowsPegging)
         | (FLOODED        && medium.flooded)

  → NATIVE all pass with margin   → WORKABLE all pass
    MARGINAL one fails softly     INCOMPATIBLE otherwise

Why each refusal happens

The interesting cells are the empty ones. Each is a real horticultural fact, not a balance decision.

CropFails because
Carrota taproot forks on any obstruction — it needs deep, fine, uncompacted medium and nothing else will do
Peanutgeocarpic: pods form underground, so its pegs need something to push into. Water offers nothing.
Potatotubers need physical volume to swell into — hence soil-family media, or aeroponics used the way industry actually uses it: seed tubers, not calories
Ricewants standing water, so flood-and-drain is its native home and free-draining media are wrong
Tomato / pepperlarge root mass clogs a nutrient film; and they need anchoring a channel cannot give — plus hand pollination, since there are no insects up here
Radish, beetswelling organs: the root must be free to expand, which a film or a bare-root chamber does not permit

The media that grow no crops at all

Seven of the thirteen media in the roster do not take a crop list — they grow their own organism. This is the other half of the portfolio, and it is where the colony's protein, fats and B12 actually come from.

H9 — what the triangle means

Staples need volume, depth and time; the media that provide those are exactly the slow, heavy, forgiving ones. The fast media grow leaves — high value per gram, near-zero stored calories. So the portfolio is not merely insurance against a power failure; it is nutritionally mandatory. You cannot eat from the fast half of the farm, and you cannot react quickly with the slow half. The buffer↔speed frontier is also a calories↔responsiveness frontier, and the player stands on both.
