Prospecting · block model · shape bench

Layer Block

Four lunar strata as one cut body — regolith over megaregolith over fractured rock over intact basalt — under 6 km of ground centred on a sect, 2 km deep, with the borehole bar on the same depth axis. The block's large-scale shape is real: read from NASA's LOLA LDEM_16, the DEM the game already ships. It samples the ground every 1.9 km, so a 6 km block is about three samples across — the site's elevation, its regional tilt and the broad form are measured, and everything finer is invented. The map in the rail is the real 182 km around Tycho; click it to move the block. The dome in the middle is that sect at its real 1 km across — which is what makes every other number here a size rather than a label — and you may not drill the ground it stands on. The vertical is exaggerated, as a block diagram's always is, and the factor is printed rather than assumed. Click a layer to take off everything above it and leave it translucent; Esc puts the stack back. Fog is fog of war: under it there are no layers at all — not hidden ones, none. The column has no types and no boundaries until you go and look. What collapses it is drilling, in two decisions. Arm the rig from the DRILL dock and put the bit tip anywhere on the ground: one click spuds in — the bit sinks its own length and the handle comes to rest on the ground, which is as far as a rig goes before anyone decides anything. Then the borehole bar starts flashing, because it is the control now: click a depth on it and the string is fed down to exactly there — the handle stays on the ground and the shaft runs through it, so what you watch is the tail above the handle being used up. It scours a bowl with a rim of spoil and throws grit the colour of the bed it is cutting, then trips out and leaves a turning wireframe core barrel in the hole. Every hole improves the whole model, not a stripe of it: a great deal where it stands, a little from the far corner of the block, nothing below the depth it reached — and what you are shown is an estimate, so each hole visibly re‑fits it. Beds rise, dips shallow out, a level bed turns out to roll. At 5% fog the model is called settled and stops moving. The bar across the top is delineation — mean confidence over the whole block — running INFERRED → INDICATED → MEASURED. Taking the block apart is a MEASURED‑only act: peeling a bed you have not established reads a number off a shape you invented, so the layer controls stay locked until 95%, which is about eight well‑spread full‑depth holes. Drag the block to turn it — all four walls are cut, all four are fogged, and every hole speaks to every one of them, most to the wall it stands nearest. Esc backs out of a planted rig, keeping the shallow hole it did make.
